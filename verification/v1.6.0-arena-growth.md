# Reserve space for large live heaps

Whole-project IR generation keeps a large checked source graph live while
allocating many short-lived records. Extending that heap by the same 1 MiB
after each unsuccessful collection causes repeated scans of the retained graph.

Keep the original 1 MiB minimum for small programs. In the normal runtime,
choose an extension of at least one quarter of the mapped heap, capped at
16 MiB for that reserve preference. A larger requested object still determines
its own minimum extent. Existing overflow, page rounding and allocation-failure
checks remain in force. Explicit internal arena-unit or cap controls retain
their fixed geometry, so small-heap stress and independent partition proofs
keep exercising their original configurations.

This changes reserve capacity only. First-fit selection, block splitting,
free-list order, precise roots, descriptor traversal and collection before a
growth attempt remain unchanged.

## Operational regression

The fixed fixture in tests/fixtures/v160-live-arena.mgn retains one million
cells in a persistent list, then allocates and drops another million cells.
Its exit checks preserve the list, first element and last temporary value.

An independent emitted-image observation reads allocation, collection and
mapped-byte counters. Both normal policy and the fixed-1-MiB control perform
3,000,002 allocations. The normal policy must collect at least once, at most
48 times, and keep mapped space at most 160 MiB. The old fixed geometry must
exceed the collection bound, demonstrating that the observation detects the
original repeated-scan behavior.

Focused results: normal growth performs 21 collections with 109,879,296 mapped
bytes; the fixed control performs 178 collections with 96,468,992 mapped bytes.
These are internal operational measurements, not adopted wall-time budgets.

A fresh cumulative suite, immutable seed and dependent compiler regeneration
are required before this change supplies accepted bootstrap evidence.
