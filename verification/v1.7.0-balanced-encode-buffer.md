# Balanced pending encoding fragments

The bounded encoder buffer used repeated concatenation of its growing byte
prefix. This limited allocation counts but repeatedly copied the prefix and
requested many different allocation sizes during whole-compiler encoding.

Keep the pending fragments in order, with their byte count bounded by 4,096.
At flush, concatenate adjacent pairs in rounds and carry an unpaired final
fragment unchanged. A fragment participates in at most the ceiling of log2 of
the fragment count rounds. Each round copies at most the pending byte count;
there are at most 4,096 nonempty pending fragments. Large fragments continue
directly into the existing carry tree. Empty fragments remain no-ops.

Native controls verify every output byte for direct carry, the frozen former
flat-prefix buffer, and the balanced buffer. The large balanced case retains
the existing one-million allocation bound and adds a 64 MiB allocated-byte
bound. The flat-prefix control exceeds 128 MiB despite satisfying the older
allocation-count bound. These are structural controls, not formal time or RSS
acceptance budgets.

The independent encoder oracle covers odd pair counts, empty and singleton
buffers, one-byte fragments across the 4,096-byte boundary, large fragments,
complete ELF bytes and unchanged invalid-record diagnostics. Whole-generation
and final version acceptance remain separate gates.
